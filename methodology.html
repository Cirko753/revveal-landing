Methodology

How Revveal measures a TikTok creator.

What Revveal measures, how to read it, and what the method can't tell. A number whose origin you don't understand doesn't help you decide.

01The posts we read: the latest, not the best

An analysis reads the creator's latest posts, in the order they were published. Not their most popular ones: a selection ranked by views almost never contains sponsored content, and can span years. Recent posts show what the creator publishes now, sponsored posts included.

Among French beauty creators, those posts cover about ten months of activity at the median. The creator page always shows the period actually covered and the date of the reading, which can be run again for fresher numbers.

02Spotting a sponsored post

No public TikTok data says whether a post was paid for. Revveal therefore reads what the creator writes: the partnership mentions in their captions and hashtags, such as #ad, “paid partnership”, a discount code, or the French “Collaboration commerciale”, across the common languages.

Hashtags are matched word for word: #ad doesn't fire on #adventure, nor #pub on #pubg. A post wrongly counted as sponsored would skew the creator's whole gap.

The verdict reads “Likely sponsored”, never a certainty. A paid post the creator doesn't flag in the caption goes unnoticed.

03Computing the sponsored gap

Sponsored posts are compared with the organic posts of the same creator, over the same period. Two measures:

Views, as a median

sponsored views (median) ÷ organic views (median)

The median rather than the mean: otherwise a single post that went viral, on either side, would be enough to flip the verdict.

Engagement, as an average per post

a post's engagement = (likes + comments + shares + saves) ÷ views

The gap score

score = average of the two ratios, sponsored ÷ organic

Both count, because a brand buys reach and interaction: keeping one while losing the other is not “holding up”. The score reads as a percentage of organic performance:

ScoreVerdict shown
100% and aboveSponsored posts outperform organic ones
85 to 100%Sponsored posts hold up
60 to 85%Moderate drop
Below 60%Sharp drop

Not enough sponsored posts, no score. The page says there isn't enough to go on rather than show a number no data supports.

Revveal also keeps track of the sponsored posts it has already seen for a creator. When they fall within the period shown, they count towards the gap, each compared with the organic posts of its own time: a creator whose audience has doubled since doesn't seem to collapse on sponsored posts.

04A worked example

A French skincare creator with about 380,000 followers, analysed in October 2026 (handle hidden), with 8 sponsored posts among her recent ones:

MeasureOrganicSponsoredRatio
Views (median)99k45k0.45
Engagement (average)14.6%8.2%0.56

score = (0.45 + 0.56) ÷ 2 ≈ 51% → sharp drop

On a sponsored post, this creator keeps about half of what she usually does. Her overall engagement rate would have looked excellent.

05The other indicators

The reliability score, out of 100

An overall marker, to compare creators at a glance. It combines the creator's engagement against that of creators in the same category, the quality of their audience, their fake follower risk and how steady their views are. The comparison is made within the category, with the sample size shown: a beauty engagement rate is not judged like a gaming one.

Fake follower risk

An index built from signals found on inflated accounts: views out of line with the follower count, engagement abnormally low for the audience, comments that don't follow the likes, numbers too regular to be natural. It is a set of clues, not proof of bought followers.

Audience quality

The share of viewers who do more than watch. Saves and shares count on top of likes and comments: they say a piece of content was genuinely useful.

06How creators are picked for a campaign

Revveal assigns each creator a category (beauty, fashion, fitness…) from their own content: what they actually talk about, not the word that found them. When nothing stands out clearly enough, the creator stays “unclassified” rather than being misfiled.

A campaign only receives creators relevant to the brand. An off-topic account is never added to reach a number: if the niche doesn't hold enough creators, the purchase window says so before payment.

07What the method can't tell

  • No official marker. Detection reads captions: an undisclosed partnership isn't seen.
  • Few creators publish sponsored content. Among randomly drawn French beauty creators, only one in ten posted enough of it to measure a gap. For the others, the page says so.
  • Public data only. No private account statistics.
  • Clues, not guarantees. A past gap describes past campaigns; a different brief, product or format can change the outcome.

Internal settings (marker lists, weights, thresholds) evolve with the database and are not detailed here.

08Data and creators

Revveal only reads public content and never publishes a list or a profile of any creator. A creator can ask to be removed from the database: the steps are in the privacy policy.
