TikTok beauty in France: what happens to the audience when the post is sponsored.

We compared French beauty creators' sponsored posts with their usual ones, across their recent posts. The typical result: a sharp drop. But not for everyone.

Measured on 22 French beauty creators with at least two sponsored and two organic posts among their recent ones. A small sample, made up mostly of creators who post sponsored content regularly: the ones brands look at. Method and sample.

Finding 1

Nine creators out of ten don't post enough sponsored content to be judged on it.

Out of 60 French beauty creators drawn at random, just over one in five had published at least one sponsored post among their recent ones. Only one in ten had published two or more, the minimum to measure a gap.

Those recent posts cover about ten months of activity at the median. For most creators, then, the engagement rate you see contains no paid content at all: it tells you how the audience reacts to what creators post for themselves, not for a brand.

Sponsored content becomes more common above 100,000 followers: one creator in three posts some.

Finding 2

When the post is paid, the audience almost always disengages.

Among creators who post sponsored content regularly, engagement drops on paid posts for 19 out of 22. For nearly one in two, it is more than halved. Views follow the same path: −43% at the median.

That is the gap an overall engagement rate doesn't show: it mixes every post, and the best ones, nearly always organic, pull it up.

The number that matters to a brand is the one on paid posts.

Finding 3

One creator in three does as well on sponsored posts, and the average hides it.

7 creators out of 22 do as well or better on sponsored posts than on organic ones, views and engagement together. Same niche, same country, opposite results.

And the two numbers don't move together: 5 of those 7 creators lose more than half their engagement on paid posts, but gain enough views to make up for it. A creator can reach more people on a sponsored post while getting fewer of them to react.

Finding 4

Two sponsored posts in three use the wording French law requires.

Since the French law of 9 June 2023, commercial influencer content must carry the mention “Publicité” (advertising) or “Collaboration commerciale” (commercial collaboration). In the captions of the sponsored posts we spotted, two in three use one of them, mostly “collaboration commerciale”.

The remaining third flag the partnership differently: #ad, #collab, “partenariat”, a discount code. These figures cover captions only: the label TikTok itself shows on a video is not part of the data.

For a brand

What it changes before you pick a creator.

  • Don't judge on the overall rate

    For most creators, it contains no paid post at all. It describes their audience when they speak for themselves.

  • Look at their sponsored posts

    Compare them with their organic posts from the same period. The gap calculator does it from a few numbers.

  • Find the ones who hold up

    One creator in three does as well or better on sponsored posts. Check on what: reach, engagement, or both.

Method and sample

Where these numbers come from.

Revveal reads each creator's recent posts (their 35 latest public posts, about ten months at the median), spots the sponsored ones from their captions, then compares views at the median and engagement as an average per post. The calculation and its limits are explained on the methodology page.

  • Finding 1: 60 French beauty creators drawn at random on 12 October 2026 from those Revveal tracks, 20 per size band.
  • Findings 2 and 3: 22 creators whose gap is measurable (at least two sponsored and two organic posts): 6 from the random draw, 16 picked among creators who post sponsored content regularly.
  • Finding 4: the 115 sponsored posts from French beauty creators spotted by Revveal as of 29 October 2026. A sponsored post with no mention at all in its caption isn't spotted, so it can't appear in this count.
  • Sponsored posts are detected, not declared: the creator page marks them “likely sponsored”. No creator is named here; Revveal only publishes aggregates.
