Finding

Per view, big accounts engage more. Per follower, it's the other way round.

Measured against views, median engagement rises with account size: 4.9% below 10,000 followers, 9.3% above 100,000. Measured against followers, it goes the other way: 4.7% for small accounts, 1.1% for large ones.

The reason: on TikTok, a video is mostly shown to people who don't follow the creator. A small account gets, at the median, almost as many views as it has followers; an account above 100,000 followers, about a seventh. Dividing by followers therefore mostly measures reach, not how the people who saw the video reacted.

Before comparing two rates, check what they divide by: views or followers.

What the rate doesn't say

A good rate says nothing about what happens when you pay.

For most beauty creators, the rate you see contains no paid post at all: only a little over one in five has published sponsored content among their recent posts. It describes their audience when they speak for themselves.

On paid posts, engagement drops by 46% at the median among those who post sponsored content regularly. Yet nearly one in three does as well as on organic posts. The TikTok beauty barometer details these figures.

Look at their sponsored posts before you sign, not just their rate.

For a brand

Three reflexes before judging an engagement rate.

  • Compare with their size

    6% engagement is good below 10,000 followers and low above 100,000. Use the grid for their size band.

  • Check what is divided

    The same account can show 9% per view and 1% per follower. Two tools that don't divide by the same thing can't be compared.

  • Look at their paid posts

    The overall rate doesn't say what their audience does when you are the one paying. Compare their sponsored posts with their organic ones.

Questions

The TikTok engagement rate, plainly.

What is a good engagement rate on TikTok?

It depends on the size of the account. Among French beauty creators, the median engagement rate per view is 4.9% between 1,000 and 10,000 followers, 6.3% between 10,000 and 100,000, and 9.3% above 100,000. Above the median for their size, a creator is good; in the top quarter, very good.

How do you calculate a TikTok engagement rate?

For each post: (likes + comments + shares + saves) ÷ views × 100. Then the average over several recent posts, leaving sponsored posts out, since they often do less. The calculator on this page does it for you.

Why divide by views rather than followers?

On TikTok, a video is mostly shown to people who don't follow the creator. Dividing by followers mostly measures reach: a small account gets, at the median, almost as many views as followers, an account above 100,000 followers about 14%. Dividing by views measures how the people who saw the video reacted, which is what a brand cares about.

Why do other sources give such different figures?

Because they don't measure the same thing: per view or per follower, with or without shares and saves, on brands or on creators, on the best videos or on the most recent ones. Before comparing two rates, check those four points.

Does the engagement rate drop on sponsored posts?

Usually, yes: by 46% at the median among French beauty creators who post sponsored content regularly. But nearly one in three does as well as on organic posts. The barometer has the details.

Method and sample

Where these figures come from.

Revveal reads each creator's recent posts (their 35 latest public posts, about ten months of activity at the median), sets aside the sponsored posts spotted in their captions, and computes each post's engagement. The calculation is explained on the methodology page.

  • Sample: 60 French beauty creators drawn at random on 12 October 2026 from those Revveal follows, 20 per account size. It is the same draw as finding 1 of the barometer.
  • A creator's engagement: the average, over their recent organic posts, of (likes + comments + shares + saves) ÷ views. Benchmark for a size: the median of creators of that size; “low” ends at the first quartile, “very good” starts at the third.
  • Views per follower: median views of their organic posts ÷ followers on the day of the draw. Engagement per follower follows from it: an approximation of interactions per post relative to followers.
  • Limits: a small sample per size, so benchmarks, not norms. The “100,000 and up” band goes up to several million followers. Public data only; no creator is named here.
